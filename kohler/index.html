Ramp

Every role requires ramp. Here's where I'm actively building.

Facilitating at program scale

I have been in the room for seven quarterly plannings and roughly 40 release cycles, so I know precisely how those events are built. I have participated in them, not run them.

In motion: shadowing then co-facilitating before owning — see the 90-day plan below.

Flow metrics and probabilistic forecasting

I work fluently in velocity, burn-up, burndown, and defect escape rate. I am deliberately building the flow vocabulary — cycle time, throughput, work item age, WIP — because averaging velocity hides the variance that actually breaks dates.

In motion: currently studying flow metrics and multi-team Scrum patterns.

Portfolio-level delivery reporting

I have built and delivered reporting for one product to leadership. Consolidating across a portfolio is new.

In motion: one of my questions for you is how Kohler does this today.

First 90 days

Days 1–30 · Learn the system, change nothing

  1. 1

    Meet every team; understand topology, size, and ownership

  2. 2

    Attend every ceremony as an observer

  3. 3

    Map cross-platform dependencies across Commerce, Search, Content, Personalization, and Operations

  4. 4

    Learn the release calendar, the intake path, and how demand actually reaches teams

  5. 5

    Baseline the delivery metrics that already exist — and note which ones do not

  6. 6

    Meet Product Managers and Product Analysts individually

Days 31–60 · Establish visibility

  1. 1

    Build or improve the single view of cross-team dependencies and delivery status

  2. 2

    Surface risks early enough that they are still cheap

  3. 3

    Shadow, then co-facilitate, program-level events before owning them

  4. 4

    Introduce flow metrics only where they answer a question someone is already asking

  5. 5

    Establish a predictable reporting rhythm to leadership

Days 61–90 · Own the cadence

  1. 1

    Own a full quarterly planning cycle end to end

  2. 2

    Own release readiness across platform initiatives

  3. 3

    Propose the first process improvement based on evidence gathered in the first 60 days, not on opinion imported from a previous employer

  4. 4

    Establish the say/do baseline that predictability gets measured against going forward

I would rather spend thirty days understanding why things are the way they are than thirty days changing things that were already right.

Questions for You

Frida Carolina mentioned you can go deeper on the practice and the products. These are what I am most curious about.

What I most want to understand

What is the biggest delivery problem you are hoping this hire solves in the first six months?

How many teams would I be accountable for, and does each have a dedicated Scrum Master today?

Is there a formal framework — SAFe, Nexus, or a homegrown quarterly cadence? Who facilitates quarterly planning today, and would that transfer to me?

What is the current allocation split across new build, enhancement, maintenance and tech debt, and compliance?

What delivery metrics does leadership look at today — and which ones do you wish you had?

Practice and product maturity

Is this a new role or a backfill? If a backfill, what did that person do well, and what was missing?

How do Commerce, Search, Content, Personalization, and Operations map to teams — one per capability, or shared platform teams?

Where does this role sit? Who does it report to, and who are its peers?

How mature is the product practice? Are there dedicated Product Managers and Product Analysts per area, or shared across?

Delivery mechanics

What is the release cadence for the platform, and is deployment decoupled from release — are you using feature flags?

How does demand reach the teams today? Is there a formal intake process?

Is there a separate operations or SRE function, or do product teams carry the operational load?

Technical expectations

How technical do you want this person to be — in the architecture conversations, or orchestrating around them?

Team and ways of working

What is the split between US-based and Mexico-based staff on these teams, and what are the timezone expectations?

What does the Monterrey office actually do, and how often do teams gather in person?

What does success look like for this role at 90 days?
